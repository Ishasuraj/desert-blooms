# Desert Blooms Website — Design Direction

## Three Initial Directions

### Theme Name: Desert Botanical Editorial
Very Brief Intro: A warm, tactile landscape identity that pairs Gulf desert tones with lush planting imagery and editorial typography. It positions Desert Blooms as a thoughtful, premium outdoor partner rather than a generic contractor.
Probability: 0.03

### Theme Name: Contemporary Garden Utility
Very Brief Intro: A clean, highly structured service-led interface with practical navigation, crisp photography, and clear conversion pathways. It emphasizes reliability and straightforward project communication.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Oasis Afterlight
Very Brief Intro: A dusk-toned, atmospheric direction using deep botanical greens, warm clay accents, and slow ambient transitions. It would feel cinematic and memorable, but is intentionally less literal for a service business.
Probability: 0.02

## Chosen Direction: Desert Botanical Editorial

### Design Movement
Contemporary botanical editorial: a blend of landscape architecture portfolios, Gulf modernism, and tactile natural-material design. The site should feel composed, calm, and rooted in place.

### Core Principles
1. **Cultivated, not decorative:** Every visual detail should suggest care, expertise, and a living relationship between soil, water, and built space.
2. **Warm restraint:** Use a quiet natural palette and strong typographic hierarchy instead of loud gradients, excessive rounded cards, or generic tech styling.
3. **Editorial asymmetry:** Prefer offset compositions, oversized type, narrow text columns, and overlapping image moments over a centered marketing template.
4. **Kuwait-aware practicality:** Present landscaping as climate-conscious outdoor infrastructure: shade, irrigation, planting, maintenance, and agricultural care.

### Color Philosophy
The palette takes its emotional cue from Kuwait at the meeting point of sand and planted life: limestone and sun-baked sand establish trust and warmth; deep date-palm green signals expertise and longevity; terracotta adds a human, crafted accent; pale sage keeps the interface breathable. The signature color is **Oasis Green `#315842`**, a grounded green that feels ownable without becoming overly rustic.

### Layout Paradigm
Use a scroll-led editorial narrative. The hero begins with a left-aligned text block and a large offset landscape image, followed by a slim “field note” rail, a staggered services composition, an asymmetrical project collage, and a contact section that feels like a handoff from screen to site visit. Avoid making every section a symmetrical three-column grid.

### Signature Elements
- A slim vertical “FIELD NOTE / KUWAIT” label rail that appears beside major sections.
- Terracotta rule marks and hand-drawn-style leaf/dune line motifs used sparingly as section punctuation.
- Image frames with gentle organic corner cuts rather than uniform rounded cards.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like a quiet physical response: buttons press slightly, images lift or reveal a warmer crop, and navigation states become more legible without theatrics. Service items can reveal a short detail line on hover/focus. Every interactive element must remain clear and usable on touch devices.

### Animation
Use short, purposeful motion under 300ms for buttons, links, filters, and image hovers. Use staggered 40–70ms entrance delays for service rows and project cards. The hero image may drift very subtly on scroll, while decorative line motifs can draw in once. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling non-essential transforms and entrance effects.

### Typography System
Use **DM Serif Display** for expressive section titles and **Manrope** for body copy, navigation, labels, and calls to action. Headlines should use generous line height and occasional italic emphasis for botanical warmth. Body text should stay compact and highly legible. Use uppercase micro-labels with increased tracking for service metadata and location cues.

### Brand Essence
Desert Blooms creates considered landscapes and agricultural environments for Kuwait clients who want outdoor spaces to thrive with intention, beauty, and practical care. Personality: **grounded, attentive, quietly confident**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines sound observant and assured, never inflated. CTAs are direct invitations to begin a site conversation. Microcopy is warm, specific, and useful.

Example headline: “Make room for a better kind of outdoors.”

Example CTA: “Plan a garden with us.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Pair a restrained uppercase wordmark with a standalone symbol: a curved desert dune line merging into a sprouting leaf and small sun arc. The symbol should work independently as a favicon, social avatar, and mobile header mark.

### Signature Brand Color
**Oasis Green `#315842`** — a deep planted green inspired by shade beneath date palms and the feeling of a landscape that has been carefully established.

## Prototype Scope

The first public prototype includes a responsive home page with: hero and enquiry CTAs, company introduction, service categories, selected project placeholders, working approach, company facts, contact details for Al Sharq in Kuwait, a WhatsApp enquiry action, and a concise enquiry form. The cost estimator, testimonials/reviews, admin dashboard, analytics, database, and live form submission are intentionally excluded for this iteration.

The generated imagery currently serves only as visual stand-ins until the company provides approved project photographs. The image slots are structured so they can be replaced later without changing the page layout.

## Style Decisions

All system and fallback pages use the Desert Botanical Editorial palette and voice: limestone and sand surfaces, Oasis Green `#315842`, terracotta accents only, DM Serif Display headlines, and Manrope utility text. Error states use calm garden-path wayfinding language and a direct invitation back to the main site. Blue, red-alert, generic app-card, and default SaaS empty-state patterns are outside the brand language.
